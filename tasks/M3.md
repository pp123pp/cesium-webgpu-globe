# M3 — V0.3 global spherical volumetric clouds

**Prerequisite:** Sun and atmosphere working from M2.

## Tasks

1. Define a curved cloud layer tied to Earth radius and geographic location; verify ray entry/exit in orbit, high altitude and near ground.
2. Generate procedural global coverage, wind advection and spatially varying density/3D noise.
3. Build volumetric integration with transmittance, anisotropic scattering, cloud self-shadow and sunlight edge/silver lining.
4. Fade from a clear far orbit to local cloud banks on approach anywhere on Earth. Preserve the cloud field across panning, revisits and in-cloud traversals. Coverage zero is clear at every altitude.
5. Improve temporal behavior (reprojection/history only if evidence shows it is required); test pan/zoom/fast flight and day-night progression.
6. Capture A11–A13 with correct cloud silhouette, thickness, lighting and stable motion.

## Planned visual enhancements

The user [accepted the cloud appearance baseline](../coordination/evidence/M3/cloud-appearance/README.md) on 2026-11-05. **M3 remains in progress until all five enhancements below are implemented and individually pass user visual acceptance.** Work through the rows in order and record each acceptance with its evidence. M4 owns shadows cast onto the globe and terrain, and changes to surface lighting.

| Step | Status | Effect | Visual acceptance |
| ---- | ------ | ------ | ----------------- |
| 1 | Planned | Use sky light for cloud ambient color and approximate multiple scattering inside thick clouds. | Hold the cloud field, camera and exposure fixed while advancing the Sun from noon to sunset. The shaded side takes on the sky color and retains visible depth. Warm light and silver lining follow the illuminated thin edges. |
| 2 | Planned | Shape distinct cloud types. Start with cumulus and stratocumulus, then evaluate a separate thin, high cirrus layer. | Compare presets from the same pose. Cumulus has rounded towers and a flatter base. Stratocumulus spreads into banks with gaps. Evaluate cirrus by looking through a low-cloud gap for fine streaks visibly above it. |
| 3 | Planned | Refine aerial perspective on distant clouds and twilight lighting across cloud altitudes. | Look horizontally across the cloud sea. Nearby banks retain detail while distant banks lose contrast towards the horizon. At dusk, higher clouds can still catch warm sunlight after lower clouds enter shade. |
| 4 | Planned | Add gradual cloud growth and erosion alongside wind transport. | Record the same cloud for tens of seconds from a fixed pose, then fly past it. Its crown grows and edges dissipate while the bank remains recognizable and moves with the wind, without popping or swimming during flight. |
| 5 | Planned | Add morning mist and cloud-gap light shafts in the air. | With a low Sun, pan sideways under broken clouds. Shafts remain aligned with cloud openings and sunlight. Close the gap and the corresponding shaft fades. Evaluate the air effect independently of terrain lighting. |

For each step, capture matched baseline and candidate views on the local physical GPU. Keep the camera, date, wind, coverage, exposure and quality fixed except for the variable under review. Include a short sequence for moving effects. Record the adapter and GPU cost at the same resolution and quality when render cost changes. The [M3 timing evidence](../coordination/evidence/M3/README.md#performance-and-resources) provides a cloud-only reference for the recorded revision.

## Non-goals

Terrain cloud shadows (M4), realistic meteorological API, rain and snow.

## Parallelism

Noise generation, scene fixtures and physics reference analysis can be separate. **Final cloud pass, depth compositing and temporal resource integration require single ownership.**

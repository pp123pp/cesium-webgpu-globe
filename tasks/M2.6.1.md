# M2.6.1 — Solar Visual Polish

**Status:** B1 visual acceptance was confirmed by the user on 2026-11-03. B2 implementation is active on `feat/m2-6-1-b2-solar-halo-corona`, stacked on the accepted B1 development branch. B3–B5 remain planned and unauthorized.
**Prerequisite:** M2.6 merged and A07–A10 accepted (PR #18, merge commit `cf4f56cebd7ee37f761bb6668770d78c5081533e`).  
**Scope:** Visual refinement of the existing M2.6 solar rendering only.

## 1. Objective

Improve the visual quality of the Sun using the solar artwork, texture pipeline, shader controls and HDR composition already integrated by M2.6.

The target is to reduce the visibly procedural / decorative appearance of the golden radial starburst, improve the depth and continuity of the halo / corona, and create a coherent transition from the bright solar disk to its surrounding glow.

This is a polish task, not a redesign of the physical Sun model or a new post-processing framework. M2.6 A07–A10 remains accepted; M2.6.1 adds a separate visual-quality gate and does not retroactively invalidate M2.6 acceptance without a demonstrated regression.

## 3. Required visual improvements

### 2.1 Solar disk and highlight transition

- Preserve scene-time Sun direction, apparent angular size, physical disk shading and WGS84 / terrain visibility.
- Keep the disk boundary readable with a bright near-white core and restrained warm transition at the edge.
- Avoid a flat white dot pasted over a separate glow, abrupt cutout edges, excessive clipping or a saturated yellow / orange core.
- Exposure changes must reveal controlled highlight roll-off; exposure increase is not a substitute for improving the solar artwork.

### 2.2 Radial Sun Rays

- Retain the current authored multi-texture approach; do not replace it with a purely analytic regular starburst.
- Reduce evenly spaced / repeated spokes, dotted spokes, hard radial cutoffs and the appearance of a decorative badge.
- Use controlled variation in ray length, width, opacity, density and scale. The result should read as subtle solar rays rather than a uniform spiky disk.
- Avoid aliasing, moiré, flicker, harsh thin lines and unstable sampling at different viewport sizes and Sun angular sizes.
- Rays remain anchored to the true projected Sun position and scene time, never to a fixed screen coordinate.

### 2.3 Solar Halo and Corona

- Establish at least three coherent scales: compact inner glow, soft intermediate halo and broad low-intensity outer falloff.
- Avoid a clearly visible perfect ring, abrupt texture boundary, uniform orange disc or hard circular mask.
- Corona detail should be irregular and multi-scale, with restrained directional / filament-like variation. It must not obscure the solar disk or resemble a noisy particle cloud.
- Keep the outer halo low contrast against black space while retaining enough visibility at normal exposure.
- Do not imply that the authored corona texture is a physically simulated solar magnetic-field model; it is a bounded visual representation.

### 2.4 HDR Bloom and color

- Preserve the existing linear-HDR → single ACES tone-map → sRGB output order.
- Tune extraction threshold, blur radius / scale and intensity for smooth falloff; avoid visible low-resolution squares, hard boundaries or excessive haze.
- Preserve the solar highlight core and black-space contrast. Do not wash out stars, erase the disk boundary or brighten unrelated parts of the frame.
- Keep the core predominantly neutral white / warm white with restrained golden outer color. Avoid excessive orange saturation and color banding.
- Bloom must continue to respect Earth and real terrain occlusion; no post-blur leakage is allowed.

### 2.5 Atmospheric and scene integration

- Keep atmospheric scattering / attenuation as the source of atmospheric transport; solar artwork must not replace or double-count physical atmosphere.
- Keep halo, rays and bloom coherent at high orbit, low orbit, orbital sunrise, low-Sun horizon and ground views.
- Preserve partial and full solar occultation. A fully hidden Sun must not leave visible solar artwork or Bloom leaking through Earth / terrain.
- Camera motion, time progression, resize and exposure changes must not cause popping, swimming, sudden scale changes or distracting temporal instability.

## 4. Explicit scope exclusions

M2.6.1 does **not** include:

- Cinematic Lens Ghosts / optical ghost chains.
- Lens Dirt overlays.
- Anamorphic horizontal streaks.
- Exaggerated cinematic Lens Flare.
- Volumetric clouds, cloud shadows or cloud lighting.
- Detailed astronomical star catalogs.
- A new general-purpose post-processing framework, full RenderGraph or PBR engine.
- Automatic exposure or a change to the established manual exposure model.
- Changes to the WebGL renderer or its output behavior.

Any future Lens Flare task must be separately scoped; it is not part of this polish task.

## 5. Engineering constraints

- Reuse M2.6 solar texture assets, deterministic GPU payload generation, existing shader bindings and resource ownership where practical.
- Prefer tuning / improving existing effects and assets over adding new subsystems or duplicating textures.
- Keep physical Sun direction, disk visibility, WGS84 intersection, scene depth, real terrain occlusion and atmospheric attenuation authoritative.
- Preserve linear-HDR composition and the single ACES / sRGB output path.
- Keep per-effect controls / diagnostic isolation available for validation. Do not add public demo controls unless separately justified.
- Preserve optional-asset fallback, failed-upload cleanup, asynchronous-load cancellation, resize behavior, device-loss handling and idempotent initialization / destruction.
- This requirements-only change does not modify engine code. Runtime implementation requires a separate explicit M2.6.1 development task.
- Follow `AGENTS.md`: batch related edits, run focused checks first, and do not repeat full regression after every small edit. Required final gates remain mandatory when implementation is ready.

## 6. Visual acceptance criteria

M2.6.1 is accepted only when all criteria below are supported by actual WebGPU canvas captures and recorded observations.

| ID | Scenario | Passing criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SVP-01 | Reference orbital Sun | Solar disk, inner glow, halo and corona form a coherent layered source; no obvious decorative badge / dotted regular starburst. |
| SVP-02 | Rays detail | Rays have controlled non-uniform variation; no harsh spokes, obvious repetition, moiré, aliasing or unstable shimmer at tested resolutions. |
| SVP-03 | Halo / corona | Multi-scale falloff is smooth; corona has restrained irregular detail; no hard ring, abrupt texture edge or noisy particle appearance. |
| SVP-04 | HDR and exposure | Capture exposure 0.5, 1.0 and 2.0 at comparable views. Disk edge and black-space contrast remain legible; high-exposure tradeoffs are documented. |
| SVP-05 | Solar elevation | Compare high-orbit, low-orbit, orbital-limb / sunrise, low-Sun horizon and ground-facing views. Effects remain coherent and appropriately attenuated. |
| SVP-06 | Earth / terrain occultation | Test visible, partial, fully Earth-hidden and real-mesh-terrain-hidden Sun. No rays, corona, halo or Bloom leak through occluders. |
| SVP-07 | Temporal stability | Capture a fixed state repeatedly and a minimum 120-frame camera / time progression. No obvious popping, texture swimming, flashes or discontinuous intensity changes. |
| SVP-08 | Resolution / resize | Verify at 1920×1080 and 1280×720 (or equivalent supported viewports), including resize and off-axis Sun positions. No visible low-resolution Bloom boundary or ray instability. |
| SVP-09 | Effect isolation | Save same-state baseline and per-effect-disabled comparisons for rays, soft halo, wide halo, corona, tint and Bloom. Each retained effect has a distinct, intentional contribution. |
| SVP-10 | Performance / lifecycle | Compare GPU timestamp measurements on the same adapter, resolution and scene against M2.6 evidence. Report mean and p95; explain material regressions. Repeated rendering, resize, destroy and recreate must not grow native resource counts. |

### Evidence package

Record exact commit and bundle identity; GPU adapter / vendor, browser, viewport and exposure; same-camera / same-time baseline and polished captures; per-effect isolation; visible / partial / fully occulted Sun; high-orbit, low-orbit, limb, horizon and ground views; 120-frame temporal sequence and resize results; GPU timestamp mean / p95 and native resource counters; concise manual observations and known limitations.

Pixel-identical matching to a reference is **not** required. Evaluate comparable camera positions, scene time, exposure, viewport and provider state. Harmless image differences may be accepted with evidence; functional regressions, occlusion leaks and resource leaks may not.
